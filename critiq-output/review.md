# Critique: coding-agent access draft

## VERDICT
Ready for author review as a randomized study proposal, not a completed experiment. The study separates phase minutes, patch quality, and delayed same-person transfer.

## STRUCTURAL DIAGNOSIS
The abstract identifies randomized access and its limited scope. The workflow now displays the arms and two measurements; it does not imply either arm performed better.

## METHODOLOGICAL REALITY
Coders can infer assignment from tool use. The revised methods freeze purpose and segmentation rules and call for balanced, independent holdout coding. Validate the coding scheme and matched task variants before recruitment.

## STATISTICAL INTEGRITY
Fewer capped minutes can reflect early quitting, not efficiency. The revision separates stopping, completion and instrumentation loss; retains assigned participants; and specifies what constitutes the joint phase-shift pattern. Plan study size and follow-up attrition sensitivity before locking the protocol. Delayed transfer remains secondary.

## RESULTS & DISCUSSION
No causal historical or workforce-wide conclusion follows from this present-day controlled comparison. The related literature addresses immediate comprehension or a different developer's handoff, not the same person's delayed transfer.

## WRITING AS DESIGN
Retained the author's standalone title and made proposed status explicit up front. Figure 1 labels arms, measures and time points in text without depending on color.

## ETHICAL SCREENING
Institutional guidance and any required approvals precede recruitment. No real employer code or credentials, no scores used for grades or employment. Specify retention and logging boundaries before actual data collection.

## RANKED IMPROVEMENTS
1. [MAJOR, revised] Address phase coding bias with balanced holdout, agreed segmentation and participant-minute agreement.
2. [MAJOR, revised] Distinguish capped effort, successful completion and quality; regard the delayed task as a separate secondary outcome.
3. [MAJOR, study] Finalize tenure and exclusions, baseline strata, bounded logging, and minimum meaningful differences before recruitment.
4. [MINOR, revised] Replace the initial table-only workflow with a split/merge diagram and repair stale pinned methods links.

## WHAT WORKS
The duplicate-notification example tests all six phases and makes the distinction between learning why a bug occurs and checking whether a fix works concrete.
